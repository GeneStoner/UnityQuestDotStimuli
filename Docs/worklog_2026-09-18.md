# Work log — 2026-09-17 and 18
*What was found, what was fixed, what is still open.*

## Bugs found and fixed

| Problem | Effect on the data | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The scene asked the headset for **60 Hz**, which the Quest 3 does not support. The request was refused and the headset stayed at its 72 Hz default | **Every session before 2026-09-17 ran at 72 Hz.** Translations were displayed across 5 frames in 71 ms rather than 7 frames in 78 ms — about 9% short. Nothing in the data revealed it, because `PresentedDurMs` was computed from the spec's 90 Hz | `targetFPS` 60 → 90 in the scene; the controller now retries, re-asserts if the runtime drops the rate, and snaps an unsupported value to the nearest supported one |
| The scene rendered at Meta's default scale (1680 × 1760 per eye) and upscaled to the 2064 × 2208 panel | Dots softer than intended; about 20 rendered pixels per degree instead of 25 | URP render scale 1.23 (panel resolution). Judged visibly sharper in an A/B test in the headset |
| **Flicker calibration never reached the stimulus.** It was applied at startup, but `ExperimentSelector` assigns the chosen spec afterwards | Every session, both labs, ran with default colours instead of the observer's isoluminant values | Calibration is now applied in `BeginBlock`, after the spec is chosen |
| A session ended silently | An experimenter could not tell a finished session from a crash (reported by the Turkey lab) | "Experiment over" shown at the end |
| Skipping a trial was silent, and skipped attempts inflated `completed_trials` | 103 skips in one Turkey session went unnoticed; the meta file read 615 completed for 512 answered trials | In-headset notice "Trial skipped — it will be repeated later"; `completed_trials` counts answered trials only, with new `aborted_trials` and `rows_written` |
| Per-frame trajectory payload strings were built even when not logged | ~10 KB of garbage per trial, inviting a GC pause | Skipped when `writeTrajectoryPayloads` is off |

## What the data now records

Each sidecar has a `display` block: headset model, refresh rate and whether it was confirmed, render scale, MSAA, eye-texture size. Each trial logs `TransDurMsMeasured`, `TransRenderedFrames`, `MaxFrameGapMs`, `TransMaxFrameGapMs` and `StimLateFrames`. A session that ran at the wrong rate, or dropped frames, now says so in its own files.

`Tools/analyze_session.py` checks all of this in one command and reports percent correct **and d′** with standard errors.

## Findings

**The red/green asymmetry is an interaction, not a property of green.** GS, practice block (200 ms translation, no cue):

| Condition | Red field | Green field |
|---|---|---|
| Red and green together, binocular | 89.1% | **28.1%** |
| Red and green together, left eye closed | 85.9% | **60.9%** |
| Both fields red, binocular | 78.1% (both fields) | — |
| Both fields green, binocular | — | **75.0%** (both fields) |

Green on its own is fine. Green paired with red, viewed binocularly, is near chance. Closing one eye recovers most of it. Ruled out by code audit and the both-red control: translation duration, coherent fraction (50% in every condition), subfield assignment, occlusion (additive blending, depth writes and tests off — confirmed in the shader and by a headset screenshot). Equiluminance error is small for GS: a careful flicker run gives 0.668 against the 0.697 used.

**The asymmetry is old and widespread.** Across 111 archived sessions the red advantage has a median of +9.0 pp, up to +59 pp. It is largest in 3.5°-aperture experiments and near zero in the 1.65° ones.

**The cueing effect survives in both colours**: across 72 sessions, median +14.1 pp on red trials and +10.2 pp on green. So the asymmetry costs sensitivity and dynamic range, not the phenomenon.

**Optimal operating point.** For an 8-alternative task the psychometric function is steepest, and statistical power greatest, at **d′ ≈ 1.3 (about 49% correct)**. Aim for uncued 35–40%, cued 55–65%. GS's uncued cells are well placed; his cued cells are past the steep region; Afife's whole range is too low.

**Stimulus geometry confirmed from a headset screenshot**, independently of the code: aperture-to-exclusion ratio 3.22 measured against 3.18 expected, dot-free radius 1.09° against 1.1°, dot width consistent with 0.12°.

**Turkey lab.** First session verified as identical in stimulus. Cueing +9.0 pp (d′ +0.36), performance low and rising through the session, 20% of trials skipped, strong response bias (E chosen 15 times, NE 113).

## Open questions

1. **Why is green poor only when paired with red, and only binocularly?** Candidates: binocular fusion of a two-colour transparent display; asymmetric masking by the more salient field; chromatic aberration interacting with prism glasses. Next runs: both-red and red/green repeats binocularly with the current calibration; the other eye; a session without prism glasses.
2. **Frame stalls, appeared 2026-09-18 afternoon.** The both-green session dropped about one frame in five (median 8 late frames per trial, 14–19 rendered frames instead of 18) where the morning's identical block had none. Cause not yet known; see below.
3. **Lapse rate** is unmeasured and currently confounded with sensitivity. Proposal: interleave easy catch trials (~10%).
4. **Fixation** cannot be verified on a Quest 3. Open whether to add a secondary fixation task.
5. **Per-sample variance** (pinned): one session repeating 8 seeds 8 times each would measure how much of the trial-to-trial variance is stimulus sampling, and settle whether yoking stimuli across conditions is worth it.
6. **Response bias**, large in both observers, and its relation to the direction-difficulty profile.
7. **Afife's calibration** values and whether her red/green asymmetry survives a properly calibrated build.
