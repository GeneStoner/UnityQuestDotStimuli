Observer GS has a clinical tendency toward esophoria (eyes over-converge). Wears prism-corrected glasses (base-out prisms) to compensate. Double vision without glasses is rare/non-obvious but possible. VR sessions are run without glasses.

**Implication for VRDots Near/Far asymmetry**: Crossed disparity (Near plane) requires convergence relative to the fixation distance — for an esophoric observer, this may be perceptually unstable or require more active fusional vergence, making Near-plane signals noisier. Uncrossed disparity (Far plane) requires divergence, which is the direction prism correction facilitates. The Far > Near cueing asymmetry in VRDots may be partially or wholly a sensory quality difference driven by GS's vergence system rather than a property of depth attention per se.

**How to apply**: Flag all Far > Near findings as potentially observer-specific. Prioritize second observer with normal vergence before drawing general conclusions about depth-attention asymmetries. Note that the vergence-comfort confound in the fixation-plane hypothesis has a specific mechanistic account for this observer.
