## 1. Design

BothFar_005m is a critical-test experiment designed to dissociate competing accounts of the Far > Near cueing asymmetry that has appeared consistently in standard VRDots sessions. In the standard paradigm, the two transparent dot fields are placed symmetrically around fixation: one at crossed disparity (Near, −0.05m from fixation) and one at uncrossed disparity (Far, +0.05m). The standard Far advantage is therefore confounded between (a) uncrossed vs. crossed disparity per se, (b) minimum vergence demand (one field requires convergence, the other divergence relative to fixation), and (c) direction relative to the fixation plane (one field is "in front of" fixation, the other is "behind"). BothFar removes this confound by placing BOTH fields behind fixation: Less-Far at +0.05m (uncrossed, small divergence relative to fixation) and More-Far at +0.10m (uncrossed, larger divergence). Neither field crosses the fixation plane. The experiment used the standard four swap conditions (N = no swap, C = color swap, Z = depth swap, CZ = both), with `linkDepthColor=0` and no color–depth linking. Trial count: n=512 total; n=32 per cell at the finest level of analysis (swap × depth plane × cueing arm). Data notation throughout: "Less-Far" = +0.05m uncrossed disparity; "More-Far" = +0.10m uncrossed disparity.

## 3. The Central Datum: F1×F4 Dissociation

The decisive finding is the sign reversal in the UNCUED arm when depth plane changes from standard to BothFar geometry. In the standard paradigm (Near/Far straddling fixation), the UNCUED arm shows Far > Near in the same direction as the CUED arm — a pattern that supported a "structural" (trial-independent) mechanism for the asymmetry. In BothFar, CUED and UNCUED now pull in opposite directions:

- **CUED**: More-Far > Less-Far (+5.5pp, n.s. but directionally consistent with standard Far preference)
- **UNCUED**: Less-Far > More-Far (+12.3pp*)

This F1×F4 dissociation (+17.8pp total) is the experiment's primary result. The two arms do not simply respond differently in magnitude — they respond in opposite directions. This is a qualitative reversal that places strong constraints on any theoretical account.

## 4. Theoretical Interpretation

### 4.1 What BothFar rules out

The simplest extension of the fixation-plane hypothesis would be: "the structural mechanism (UNCUED bias) extends toward greater uncrossed disparity, regardless of crossing the fixation plane." Under this reading, UNCUED should prefer More-Far (+0.10m uncrossed) over Less-Far (+0.05m uncrossed), because More-Far is further behind fixation in the "preferred" direction. The data clearly contradict this prediction: UNCUED prefers Less-Far. The structural mechanism does not simply track "more uncrossed = better."

### 4.2 What BothFar supports: minimum vergence demand as the UNCUED default

The UNCUED results across both paradigms are internally consistent under a minimum-disparity account: the automatic tracking system defaults to whichever plane imposes the smallest absolute disparity from the fixation anchor, regardless of the sign (crossed vs. uncrossed) of that disparity.

- Standard paradigm: Near (−0.05m, crossed) and Far (+0.05m, uncrossed) are equidistant in disparity magnitude, but uncrossed is more stable for observer GS (esophoric) → UNCUED prefers Far.
- BothFar: Less-Far (+0.05m, uncrossed) and More-Far (+0.10m, uncrossed) differ in disparity magnitude; Less-Far imposes less vergence demand → UNCUED prefers Less-Far.

Both observations fit the rule: automatic tracking gravitates toward the plane with minimum absolute disparity from fixation (weighted by vergence comfort). No attentional mechanism is required for the UNCUED arm; the bias is entirely consistent with a sensory/vergence-driven structural baseline.

### 4.3 The CUED arm: attentional enhancement of the more distinctive plane

The CUED arm favors the more extreme depth plane in both experiments:
- Standard paradigm: CUED favors Far (+0.05m uncrossed) over Near (−0.05m crossed)
- BothFar: CUED favors More-Far (+0.10m uncrossed) over Less-Far (+0.05m uncrossed)

In both cases the preferred plane has greater perceptual distinctiveness — larger disparity magnitude and greater physical separation from the competing field. One interpretation is that the onset cue is able to capture attention more cleanly when the two planes are more separated, because figure-ground segregation is stronger. Alternatively, the attentional system may preferentially represent the more extreme depth value in a two-plane display regardless of absolute location. Notably, the CUED preference for More-Far occurs despite that plane imposing greater vergence demand — the attentional boost is large enough to overcome the vergence cost that suppresses the UNCUED arm.

### 4.4 Two opposing mechanisms

The F1×F4 dissociation reveals two components that were previously masked when they acted in concert:

1. **Structural/automatic mechanism (UNCUED arm):** Defaults to the plane with minimum vergence demand. In standard Near/Far, this happens to coincide with Far; in BothFar it coincides with Less-Far. The mechanism is not "Far preference" per se — it is "least disparity strain."

2. **Attentional mechanism (CUED arm):** Preferentially enhances the plane with greater perceptual distinctiveness/depth separation. This favors the more extreme plane (Far, More-Far) in both paradigms. The attentional boost can override the vergence cost incurred by the more extreme plane.

These forces are additive when the more extreme plane is also the more comfortable one (not achieved in either paradigm so far). They dissociate — and can oppose each other — when the more extreme plane also requires greater vergence effort, as in BothFar. The large F1×F4 interaction (+17.8pp) quantifies this dissociation.

### 4.5 Z condition: depth-swap interaction with depth plane

In the CUED arm, the Z condition (depth swap) reverses the usual More-Far > Less-Far ordering: CUED·Z·More-Far = +15.6pp vs CUED·Z·Less-Far = +28.1pp (Δ = −12.5pp, n.s. at n=32/cell). When the cued translator swaps from More-Far to Less-Far at tStart, tracking disruption is larger than when it swaps from Less-Far to More-Far. A speculative interpretation: after the swap, attention must re-establish tracking at the new depth, and re-engagement may be easier when the translator moves toward fixation (Less-Far→More-Far direction places the re-engaged target further out, but the initial re-engagement is in the "pull away from fixation" direction). This is consistent with the idea that attention extends more readily outward (away from fixation) than inward. This finding is directional only at current power.

## 5. Comparison to Standard Paradigm Data

### F1 (dot cueing)
BothFar CUED=+33.2pp is well within the range observed in DecoupledDots (+22.3pp***) and DepthSwapCtrl (+34.3pp***). F1 is robust and geometry-independent.

### F2 (depth continuity)
The depth-swap disruption in the CUED arm (~17pp, N vs Z in CUED) is comparable to DecoupledDots (~13pp GLM estimate) and DepthColorLinked (~19pp). F2 survives in BothFar and does not require straddling the fixation plane. Depth-plane object identity matters even when both planes carry the same sign of disparity.

### UNCUED arm behavior
Standard DepthSwapCtrl UNCUED (binocular): +10.4pp Far, overall. DecoupledDots UNCUED: +5.4pp. BothFar UNCUED: +5.4pp overall (+11.5pp Less-Far, −0.8pp More-Far). The UNCUED arm remains small overall, but its internal structure (Less-Far vs More-Far) is now the theoretically diagnostic quantity.

## 6. Caveats

1. **Single observer (GS).** The vergence-comfort/esophoria interpretation is motivated by GS-specific physiology. A second observer with normal vergence might show no UNCUED asymmetry, or the opposite pattern. Observer-specificity is the dominant uncertainty.

2. **Cell-level power.** n=32/cell at the finest level (swap × depth × arm). Wilson 95% CIs are approximately ±17pp. Cell-level comparisons are directional and should be treated with corresponding caution. The main UNCUED Less-Far > More-Far contrast (+12.3pp*) is at the swap-collapsed level (n=128/cell) and is the most reliable cell in the design.

3. **n=512 total.** The overall F1, F4 (marginal), and F1×F4 results have adequate power; the interaction is +17.8pp. The swap-level breakdown (Section 2.3) is at n=32/cell and should not be over-interpreted.

4. **No GLM fit reported.** The numerical summary above is descriptive (conditional proportions and differences). A formal logistic regression has not yet been fit. The interaction magnitude (+17.8pp) is a descriptive estimate and may differ slightly from a model-based AME.

## 7. Critical Next Experiments

1. **Second observer at standard geometry and BothFar geometry.** The UNCUED reversal is the key prediction of the minimum-disparity account. If a second observer (normal vergence) shows no systematic UNCUED depth-plane preference in either paradigm, the effect is entirely observer-specific (GS esophoria). If a second observer shows the same reversal pattern, the minimum-disparity account gains generality. This is the single highest priority.

2. **Direct within-session comparison: standard Near/Far vs BothFar.** A mixed design with both geometry types would provide a cleaner within-observer quantification of the F1×F4 interaction across geometries.

3. **AllNear geometry.** By symmetry, placing both fields in front of fixation (both crossed disparity) — Less-Near at −0.05m and More-Near at −0.10m — should produce the mirror-image pattern if the minimum-disparity account is correct: UNCUED should prefer Less-Near (smaller crossed disparity, less vergence strain for a non-esophoric observer; potentially ambiguous for GS). This completes the 2×2 design crossing geometry (straddle / both-far / both-near) × cueing arm.

4. **Increased power on BothFar.** Additional BothFar sessions (target n≥128/cell at swap×depth×arm level) would permit formal GLM analysis and confirm the sign of the Z-condition reversal.
