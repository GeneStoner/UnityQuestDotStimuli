# Motion in the Empty Space Between Dots: Focused Evidence
*Prepared 2026-05-30*
*Scope: continuously moving random dot patterns only — not apparent motion / discrete jumps*

Three distinct mechanisms are implicated. All PMIDs confirmed by search unless marked ⚠️.

---

## 1. Global Spatial Pooling — Motion Perception Survives Very Sparse Dot Fields

If motion coherence were computed only at dot positions, performance should degrade steeply as density drops and empty space grows. Instead, the system averages across vast empty inter-dot territory with near-ideal efficiency.

**Williams, D.W. & Sekuler, R. (1984).** Coherent global motion percepts from stochastic local motions. *Vision Research*, 24, 55–62. [PMID: 6695508]
Density had negligible effect on coherence threshold across a wide range of sparse dot fields. The first behavioral signature that the motion system pools across the empty space between dots rather than requiring a densely sampled field.

**Watamaniuk, S.N.J., Sekuler, R. & Williams, D.W. (1989).** Direction perception in complex dynamic displays: the integration of direction information. *Vision Research*, 29, 47–59. [PMID: 2773336]
Performance improved with increasing display area even as density was held low — implying the system spatially averages across a region far larger than any individual dot's footprint. Coherent motion is read out of the empty inter-dot field, not just from dot positions.

**Watamaniuk, S.N.J. (1993).** Ideal observer for discrimination of the global direction of dynamic random-dot stimuli. *Journal of the Optical Society of America A*, 10, 16–28. [PMID: 8478742]
Compared human global-direction discrimination to an ideal observer as functions of area and dot density. Increasing area improved performance beyond what density alone predicted — the system integrates over the empty space between dots, not by summing independent point-detectors.

**Watamaniuk, S.N.J. & Duchon, A. (1992).** The human visual system averages speed information. *Vision Research*, 32, 931–941. [PMID: 1604862]
Observers averaged speed across all visible dots in an RDK — integration across the whole display including empty inter-dot space — with thresholds tracking the mean rather than any local extreme. A global pooling mechanism that operates over empty regions, not just occupied pixels.

**Barlow, H. & Tripathy, S.P. (1997).** Correspondence noise and signal pooling in the detection of coherent visual motion. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 17, 7954–7966. [PMID: 9315913]
Ideal-observer model incorporating "correspondence noise" (ambiguity in matching dots across frames). Human observers are near-ideal poolers of the global signal, and performance improves with area in a way that requires integration across empty regions. Performance closely follows a model that explicitly bridges empty space between dot positions.

**Dakin, S.C., Mareschal, I. & Bex, P.J. (2005).** Local and global limitations on direction integration assessed using equivalent noise analysis. *Vision Research*, 45, 3027–3049. [PMID: 16171844]
Partitioned local noise (individual element imprecision) from global noise (spatial sampling limitations). At low densities, global noise dominates — the system must pool over large empty patches. Performance degrades when inter-element spacing exceeds the pooling kernel, not when density falls below an absolute threshold.

## 2. Trajectory Integration Through Inter-Dot Gaps

A single moving dot's successive sampled positions are linked through the interpolated path in the empty space between them — and this exceeds what any local motion detector could achieve.

**Watamaniuk, S.N.J., McKee, S.P. & Grzywacz, N.M. (1995).** Detecting a trajectory embedded in random-direction motion noise. *Vision Research*, 35, 65–77. [PMID: 7839611]
A single signal dot moved on a straight trajectory through random-direction noise dots. Observers detected it at rates up to 90% even when nearest-neighbor matching strongly predicted mismatch — the system threaded together the trajectory across inter-frame gaps. Direct evidence of spatial interpolation of motion through the empty space between a dot's sampled positions.

**Verghese, P., Watamaniuk, S.N.J., McKee, S.P. & Grzywacz, N.M. (1999).** Local motion detectors cannot account for the detectability of an extended trajectory in noise. *Vision Research*, 39, 19–30. [PMID: 10211392]
Detection improves far more with trajectory length than any local-detector model allows. The system links successive motion samples across the empty gaps between them — integration extends over space and time beyond what a purely local mechanism would see. A computational proof that inter-dot interpolation is required to explain behavior.

## 3. Motion Streaks — V1 Encodes the Empty Path Just Traversed

Fast-moving dots leave an oriented spatial smear ("streak") through the empty space they have just crossed. V1 orientation-selective neurons read direction from this streak — including at locations the dot is no longer occupying.

**Geisler, W.S. (1999).** Motion streaks provide a spatial code for motion direction. *Nature*, 400, 65–69. [PMID: 10403249]
Orientation-masking with RDKs: direction discrimination is selectively impaired by noise oriented *parallel* to the motion direction (the streak orientation). The streak extends through the space the dot has just vacated — the orientation structure in empty inter-dot space is what V1 uses for a second direction code. Proposed that this applies directly to dot fields in general.

**Geisler, W.S., Albrecht, D.G., Crane, A.M. & Stern, L. (2001).** Motion direction signals in the primary visual cortex of cat and monkey. *Visual Neuroscience*, 18, 501–516. [PMID: 11829297]
V1 neurons in cat and macaque fire most strongly when a dot moves parallel to their preferred orientation at high speeds, using both grating and random-dot stimuli. "Streak cells" respond to the oriented luminance smear left in the space a dot has just crossed. Direct neurophysiological evidence that motion streaks are represented in V1 during RDK viewing.

**Burr, D.C. & Ross, J. (2002).** Direct evidence that "speedlines" influence motion mechanisms. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 22, 8661–8664. [PMID: 12351740]
Masking with noise oriented parallel to the motion direction of an RDK selectively impaired direction discrimination; orthogonal masks had little effect. Because the mask occupies the empty space the dot has just traversed (the streak region), this is behavioral confirmation that the visual system reads direction from the orientation structure in inter-dot / post-dot empty space.

## 4. MT Neuronal Recordings — Integration Across Empty Space Within the RF

MT receptive fields are large (5°–20°) and integrate motion signals across the empty regions between sparse dots — by design and directly measured.

**Snowden, R.J., Treue, S. & Andersen, R.A. (1992).** The response of neurons in areas V1 and MT of the alert rhesus monkey to moving random dot patterns. *Experimental Brain Research*, 88, 389–400. [PMID: 1577111]
V1 neurons showed high response variability across different dot patterns at the same density; MT neurons were far more consistently direction-selective and maintained robust responses at very sparse densities. MT pools direction signals over large RFs regardless of whether those RFs are densely or sparsely filled — encoding motion in regions where most of the RF contains no dots at any given moment.

**Britten, K.H. & Heuer, H.W. (1999).** Spatial summation in the receptive fields of MT neurons. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 19, 5074–5084. [PMID: 10366640]
Small (~1°) Gabors placed at sparse positions within MT RFs, with wide empty gaps between them. MT neurons integrate motion signals from spatially separated stimuli; the empty inter-probe regions do not interrupt integration. Pairs moving in the preferred direction summed super-additively; inhibitory interactions between opposite-direction stimuli were also spatially distributed across the RF. **The most direct single-unit evidence that MT integrates motion across the empty space between sparse dots.**

**Recanzone, G.H., Wurtz, R.H. & Schwarz, U. (1997).** Responses of MT and MST neurons to one and two moving objects in the receptive field. *Journal of Neurophysiology*, 78, 2904–2915. [PMID: 9405511]
With two stimuli moving in different directions within an MT RF, neurons averaged the two directions in a vector-sum manner across the empty gap between the stimuli. Direct evidence that MT integrates motion signals over the empty inter-stimulus region rather than responding only to the occupied parts of the RF.

**Pack, C.C. & Born, R.T. (2001).** Temporal dynamics of a neural solution to the aperture problem in visual area MT of macaque brain. *Nature*, 409, 1040–1042. [PMID: 11234012]
MT neurons initially encode local (aperture-ambiguous) motion, then shift to the true global direction within ~60 ms by integrating signals from spatially disparate terminators and surface regions — including empty portions of the RF. Physiological evidence that MT computes motion across empty spatial regions in real time, progressively over tens of milliseconds.

**Pack, C.C., Gartland, A.J. & Born, R.T. (2004).** Integration of contour and terminator signals in visual area MT of alert macaque. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 24, 3268–3280. [PMID: 15056706]
MT combines terminator signals (unambiguous endpoints) with interior contour motion across empty space between them on a ~60 ms timescale. The spatial scale of integration was far smaller than the RF, confirming that MT actively fills in motion direction across empty intra-RF gaps.

**Allman, J., Miezin, F. & McGuinness, E. (1985).** Stimulus specific responses from beyond the classical receptive field. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 8, 407–430. [PMID: 3885829]
>90% of MT neurons have antagonistic surrounds 50–100× larger than their classical RFs — direction-selective and velocity-tuned. In a sparse-dot stimulus, the surround actively samples large portions of empty field. MT computes motion by comparing the RF with a vast surrounding territory, most of which is empty in typical RDK displays.

**Raiguel, S., Van Hulle, M.M., Xiao, D.K., Marcar, V.L. & Orban, G.A. (1995).** Shape and spatial distribution of receptive fields and antagonistic motion surrounds in MT/V5 of the macaque. *European Journal of Neuroscience*, 7, 2064–2082. [PMID: 8542064]
MT surround half-height radius is 3–4× the classical RF radius (up to 10×). Peak surround sensitivity is displaced *upstream* of the preferred direction — the surround probes the empty-space trajectory the moving dot will *next* traverse. Predictive sampling of empty space ahead of the dot.

## 5. Perceptual Filling-In of Motion Between Moving Elements

**Lin, Z. & He, S. (2012).** Emergent filling-in induced by motion integration reveals a high-level mechanism in filling-in. *Psychological Science*, 23, 1534–1541. [PMID: 23085642]
Four apertures at the corners of an invisible rectangle, each with local oblique motion consistent with a horizontally sliding rectangle. Observers perceived a distinct horizontal motion in the *empty region between the apertures* — motion not present locally anywhere. A MAE adapted by the emergent (not local) motion confirmed a genuine high-level motion representation in the gap. hMT+/MST feeds back to V1/V2 to fill empty space with globally integrated motion.

## Summary

| Mechanism | Best evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Density-invariant global pooling | Williams & Sekuler 1984; Watamaniuk 1993; Barlow & Tripathy 1997 | Coherence perception survives extreme sparsity; system averages over empty inter-dot space |
| Trajectory linking through gaps | Watamaniuk et al. 1995; Verghese et al. 1999 | Single dot paths interpolated across inter-frame gaps; local detectors insufficient |
| Motion streaks in V1 | Geisler 1999; Geisler et al. 2001; Burr & Ross 2002 | V1 streak cells encode the orientation trace left in the vacated path; masking the streak region disrupts RDK direction discrimination |
| MT RF summation across gaps | Britten & Heuer 1999; Recanzone et al. 1997 | MT directly integrates sparse stimuli across empty inter-dot regions; super-additive summation |
| MT temporal integration | Pack & Born 2001; Pack et al. 2004 | MT builds correct direction over ~60 ms by integrating across spatial gaps in the RF |
| MT surround — upstream sampling | Allman et al. 1985; Raiguel et al. 1995 | MT surrounds sample the empty space ahead of and around each dot |
| Perceptual filling-in | Lin & He 2012 | Vivid motion perceived in empty inter-element space; MAE confirms genuine neural representation |
