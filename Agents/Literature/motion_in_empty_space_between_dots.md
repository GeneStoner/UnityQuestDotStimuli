# Motion Assigned to Empty Space Between Dots: Evidence
*Prepared 2026-05-30*

Evidence converges from six independent lines — psychophysics, neuronal recordings, fMRI, VSD imaging, MAE studies, and theoretical/computational models. PMIDs confirmed by search where listed.

---

## 1. Motion Aftereffect Transfers to Blank / Empty Regions

The MAE is seen on featureless test fields and in the gaps between adapting dots — demonstrating that motion is encoded at a spatial scale far larger than individual dot positions.

**Weisstein, N., Maguire, W. & Berbaum, K. (1977).** A phantom-motion aftereffect. *Science*, 198, 955–958. [PMID: 929181]
Moving stripes flanking a physically empty horizontal band induced a MAE *in the empty band* — no stimulus had ever appeared there. The most direct classical demonstration that the motion system assigns motion to empty space.

**Mather, G. (1980).** The movement aftereffect and a distribution-shift model for coding the direction of visual movement. *Perception*, 9, 379–392. [PMID: 7422456]
MAE arises from a population-level shift across all directionally tuned units. Because the test field can be blank or uniform, the adapted state is expressed across spatial regions that never contained a moving dot — motion encoding is distributed over space, not tied to dot positions.

**Hiris, E. & Blake, R. (1992).** Another perspective on the visual motion aftereffect. *PNAS*, 89, 9025–9028. [PMID: 1409598]
With a dynamic noise test field (no net direction), observers see a compelling MAE even when no physically moving element occupied the adapted locations. Higher-stage motion mechanisms integrate over large spatial regions including inter-dot gaps; their adaptation is revealed on a featureless test field.

**Nishida, S. & Sato, T. (1995).** Motion aftereffect with flickering test patterns reveals higher stages of motion processing. *Vision Research*, 35, 477–490. [PMID: 7900288]
Sparse dot fields with large empty regions produce robust MAEs on uniform flickering tests, with complete interocular transfer. Adaptation occurred at cortical levels (V1/MT) with large RFs spanning inter-dot gaps — the adapted mechanism operates over the whole field including empty space.

**Snowden, R.J. & Milne, A.B. (1997).** Phantom motion aftereffects — evidence of detectors for the analysis of optic flow. *Current Biology*, 7, 717–722. [PMID: 9368753]
Adaptation to optic-flow motion in two non-adjacent quadrants produced MAEs in the *other two* completely unstimulated quadrants (~half the magnitude of direct MAEs). Large-RF mechanisms (MT/MST) populated the empty segments with motion context during adaptation; the empty space was not motion-neutral.

**Anstis, S., Verstraten, F.A.J. & Mather, G. (1998).** The motion aftereffect. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2, 111–117.
Review treating the spatial spread of MAE to empty regions between adapting dots as established psychophysical fact. MAE is spatially distributed across the entire adapted region at a scale far exceeding individual dot positions.

## 2. Motion Capture — Motion Broadcasts from Dots to Surrounding Empty Space

**Ramachandran, V.S. (1987).** Interaction between colour and motion in human vision. *Nature*, 328, 645–647. [PMID: 3614367]
Sparse moving dots near a stationary color border cause the border to appear to move with the dots — even though it carries no motion signal. Motion computed from dot positions is spatially broadcast to adjacent empty regions and features. The canonical motion-capture demonstration.

**Ramachandran, V.S. & Cavanagh, P. (1987).** Motion capture anisotropy. *Vision Research*, 27, 97–106. [PMID: 3617550]
Maps the conditions for motion capture: a sparse moving pattern attributes its motion to a superimposed stationary/incoherent pattern. Motion spreads from sparse dot locations to fill in perceived motion of the entire display, including empty regions.

**Murakami, I. & Shimojo, S. (1993).** Motion capture changes to induced motion at higher luminance contrasts, smaller eccentricities, and larger inducer sizes. *Vision Research*, 33, 2091–2107.
At small stimulus sizes, surrounding motion literally takes over the perceived motion of a stationary central region — motion is "teleported" across the empty center from surrounding dots. Size/eccentricity scaling mirrors MT receptive field scaling, implicating MT-level spatial pooling as the mechanism. ⚠️ Verify PMID independently.

## 3. Direct Neuronal Recordings — MT Integrates Across Empty Space

**Allman, J., Miezin, F. & McGuinness, E. (1985a).** Stimulus specific responses from beyond the classical receptive field. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 8, 407–430. [PMID: 3885829]
>90% of MT neurons in owl monkeys have antagonistic surrounds 50–100× larger in area than their classical RFs. These surrounds respond to motion in regions well outside (and including the empty space surrounding) any individual dot. MT neurons compute motion by comparing the RF location with a vast surrounding territory, most of which is empty in sparse dot displays.

**Allman, J., Miezin, F. & McGuinness, E. (1985b).** Direction- and velocity-specific responses from beyond the classical receptive field in the middle temporal visual area (MT). *Perception*, 14, 105–126. [PMID: 4069941]
MT surround responses are direction-selective and velocity-tuned — not just inhibitory noise. In a sparse-dot stimulus, the surround actively samples large portions of empty field. This is the mechanism by which MT solves figure-ground relative motion: it samples motion across regions where no dot currently resides.

**Britten, K.H. & Heuer, H.W. (1999).** Spatial summation in the receptive fields of MT neurons. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 19, 5074–5084. [PMID: 10366640]
Small moving Gabors placed at sparse locations within MT RFs, with wide empty gaps between them. MT neurons integrate motion signals from multiple spatially separated sparse stimuli; the empty inter-probe regions do not interrupt integration. Direct single-unit evidence that MT treats the entire RF volume — including empty space between dots — as a unified motion-sampling aperture.

**Pack, C.C., Gartland, A.J. & Born, R.T. (2004).** Integration of contour and terminator signals in visual area MT of alert macaque. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 24, 3268–3280. [PMID: 15056706]
MT neurons shift from aperture-ambiguous to veridical direction within ~60 ms by integrating terminator signals spatially distant from the contour body — across empty space. Demonstrates active motion pooling across empty regions in real time.

**Raiguel, S., Van Hulle, M.M., Xiao, D.K., Marcar, V.L. & Orban, G.A. (1995).** Shape and spatial distribution of receptive fields and antagonistic motion surrounds in the middle temporal area (V5) of the macaque. *European Journal of Neuroscience*, 7, 2064–2082. [PMID: 8542064]
MT surround half-height radius is 3–4× (up to 10×) the classical RF radius. Surround sensitivity peaks *upstream* of preferred motion direction — the surround probes the empty-space trajectory the moving dot will *next* traverse. Predictive sampling of empty space ahead of the dot.

**Born, R.T. & Bradley, D.C. (2005).** Structure and function of visual area MT. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*, 28, 157–189. [PMID: 16022593]
Comprehensive review: MT RF size (5°–20° at central eccentricities) ensures extensive integration over inter-dot empty space under normal random-dot stimulation. Center-surround organization allows MT to simultaneously represent global field motion and relative motion at boundaries by monitoring large empty territories surrounding each element.

## 4. fMRI and VSD Imaging — Early Visual Cortex Activated at Empty-Space Locations

**Muckli, L., Kohler, A., Kriegeskorte, N. & Singer, W. (2005).** Primary visual cortex activity along the apparent-motion trace reflects illusory perception. *PLOS Biology*, 3, e265. [PMID: 16018720]
Two alternating squares in apparent motion: V1 shows activation at retinotopic locations corresponding to the illusory motion path — locations with no direct retinal input at all. In bistable motion quartets, V1 activity switched sides as perceptual state changed, without any change in physical stimulus. Top-down feedback from hMT+ to V1 populates empty retinotopic loci with a motion signal.

**Jancke, D., Chavane, F., Naaman, S. & Grinvald, A. (2004).** Imaging cortical correlates of illusion in early visual cortex. *Nature*, 428, 423–426. [PMID: 15042090]
Voltage-sensitive dye imaging in cat V1: in the line-motion illusion, a traveling wave of activation propagated across the full retinotopic extent of a static bar — including locations never stimulated — indistinguishable from the wave produced by a physically moving object. The cortex generates a virtual motion signal through empty retinotopic space via lateral connections or feedback.

**Muckli, L. & Petro, L.S. (2013).** Network interactions: non-geniculate input to V1. *Current Opinion in Neurobiology*, 23, 195–201. [PMID: 23402951]
Review establishing that V5/MT feedback to V1 is among the strongest non-retinal inputs. Presenting stimuli only in the silent surround of V1 RFs reveals rich motion-related predictive content in the feedback signal, explicitly populating empty retinotopic locations with motion information.

**Agostino, C.S. et al. (2023).** Seeing and extrapolating motion trajectories share common informative activation patterns in primary visual cortex. *Human Brain Mapping*, 44, 1389–1406. [PMID: 36288211]
Multivariate pattern analysis: V1 activity during visible motion can above-chance decode the direction of a subsequently occluded trajectory at retinotopic locations corresponding to the *empty* occluded portion of the path. V1 tracks the perceived direction along the missing segment.

## 5. Motion Filling-In Beyond Stimulus Boundaries

**Maus, G.W. & Whitney, D. (2016).** Motion-dependent filling-in of spatiotemporal information at the blind spot. *PLOS ONE*, 11, e0153896. [PMID: 27100795]
Gratings drifting *into* the physiological blind spot appear longer than those drifting out of it or flickering. The motion signal propagates spatiotemporal structure into a region receiving zero retinal input — directly analogous to what may happen in empty inter-dot space when a dot moves toward it.

**Lin, Z. & He, S. (2012).** Emergent filling-in induced by motion integration reveals a high-level mechanism in filling-in. *Psychological Science*, 23, 1534–1541. [PMID: 23085642]
Local oblique motions arranged in a ring caused a distinct *horizontal* motion to emerge perceptually in the empty zones between elements — a motion present in neither the elements nor the empty region. MAEs transferable from these empty zones. Higher-level motion areas (hMT+/MST) feed back to V1/V2 to fill empty space with globally integrated motion.

## 6. Motion Spread and Position Distortion at Remote Locations

**Whitney, D. & Cavanagh, P. (2000).** Motion distorts visual space: shifting the perceived position of remote stationary objects. *Nature Neuroscience*, 3, 954–959. [PMID: 10966628]
A stationary flash near (but not overlapping) a moving pattern is perceived as displaced in the motion direction — the flash-drag effect. The motion signal spreads across the empty space between the moving dots and the flash location, distorting perceived position there. Registered in early visual cortex.

**Murakami, I. & Cavanagh, P. (1998).** A jitter aftereffect reveals motion-based stabilization of vision. *Nature*, 395, 798–801. [PMID: 9813986]
Adapting to moving random dots in one region caused the surrounding *static* region — which never contained motion — to appear to jitter. Motion signals from the dot region spread spatially into the adjacent empty territory.

**Murakami, I. & Cavanagh, P. (2001).** Visual jitter: evidence for visual-motion-based compensation of retinal slip due to small eye movements. *Vision Research*, 41, 173–186. [PMID: 11163852]
Quantified the spatial range: the jitter illusion extends well beyond the adapted area into unstimulated regions, with size specificity matching MT receptive field scaling. Spatial pooling and broadcasting of motion signals to empty surrounding regions is an MT-level mechanism.

## 7. Induced Motion — Context Assigns Motion to Empty/Stationary Regions

**Duncker, K. (1929).** Über induzierte Bewegung. *Psychologische Forschung*, 12, 180–259.
The foundational demonstration: a stationary object enclosed within a moving frame is perceived to move opposite to the frame. Motion is attributed to the stationary/empty interior purely through relative-motion context — no local motion signal present. [Not PubMed indexed.]

**Takemura, H., Ashida, H., Amano, K., Kitaoka, A. & Murakami, I. (2012).** Neural correlates of induced motion perception in the human brain. *Journal of Neuroscience*, 32, 14344–14354. [PMID: 23055505]
fMRI: hMT+ encodes the *perceived* motion of a central stationary region embedded in a moving surround, not the actual (zero) velocity. Early visual areas (V1–V3) show similar attenuated modulations, suggesting motion assignment to the empty/stationary center propagates back from hMT+ to retinotopic cortex.

## 8. Illusory Line Motion — Motion Assigned to Static Space by Attention/Prior

**Hikosaka, O., Miyauchi, S. & Shimojo, S. (1993).** Focal visual attention produces illusory temporal order and motion sensation. *Vision Research*, 33, 1219–1240. [PMID: 8333171]
A spatial attention cue preceding simultaneous onset of a full bar causes the bar to appear to unfurl from the cued end — motion drawn across entirely static spatial locations. No luminance change along the bar's extent; the visual system uses a prior attention/motion signal to construct motion across empty or uniformly-lit space.

## 9. Second- and Third-Order Motion — Empty Space Can Carry Motion Signal

**Lu, Z.-L. & Sperling, G. (1995).** The functional architecture of human visual motion perception. *Vision Research*, 35, 2697–2722. [PMID: 7483310]
The **second-order system** tracks moving contrast envelopes — can assign motion to regions with no luminance change. The **third-order (salience map) system** tracks displacement of marked figure locations; the unmarked ground (empty space) is the medium through which figure displacement is computed. Neither system requires luminance signal at the empty locations.

**Lu, Z.-L. & Sperling, G. (2001).** Three-systems theory of human visual motion perception: review and update. *JOSA A*, 18, 2331–2370. [PMID: 11551067]
Comprehensive update: the third-order system tracks salience-map displacements across the full spatial field. Any location that briefly holds figure status can contribute a motion signal; the computation explicitly traverses empty space between successive figure positions.

## 10. Motion Streaks — V1 Codes the Empty Path Just Traversed

**Geisler, W.S. (1999).** Motion streaks provide a spatial code for motion direction. *Nature*, 400, 65–69. [PMID: 10403249]
A fast-moving dot leaves an oriented spatial streak across its trajectory. V1 orientation cells respond to this streak — including locations where the dot *was* but is no longer. The spatial trail through empty space is the signal used to disambiguate direction.

**Geisler, W.S., Albrecht, D.G., Crane, A.M. & Stern, L. (2001).** Motion direction signals in the primary visual cortex of cat and monkey. *Visual Neuroscience*, 18, 501–516. [PMID: 11829297]
V1 simple cells with orientation tuning aligned to the axis of motion respond during the brief period when the dot has passed and only the trailing smear occupies their RF. V1 carries a neural signal for the trajectory path — including empty space before dot arrival — from orientation-selective responses alone.
