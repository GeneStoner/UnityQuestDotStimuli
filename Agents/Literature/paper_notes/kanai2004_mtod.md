# Kanai, Paffen, Gerbino & Verstraten (2004)

**Title:** Blindness to inconsistent local signals in motion transparency from oscillating dots
**Journal:** Vision Research 44(19):2207–2212
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.visres.2004.04.010
**PMID:** 15208007
**PDF:** Not locally available; Academia.edu PDF at https://www.academia.edu/3163939/

---

## What They Did

Introduced a novel stimulus: **Motion Transparency from Oscillating Dots (MTOD)**.

Each dot follows a sawtooth position function — it oscillates back and forth at 2.5 Hz with 0.4° amplitude (4.0°/s peak velocity). The key manipulation is the **phase** of each dot's oscillation cycle:

- **Synchronized** (synchronicity index = 1): all dots reverse direction at the same time → observers see two surfaces moving back and forth globally (oscillating percept)
- **Desynchronized / random phase** (synchronicity index = 0): each dot's reversal timing is independently randomized → observers see **two surfaces streaming past each other continuously** — classic transparent motion — despite the fact that every individual dot is locally oscillating and has zero net displacement

Display: 600 white dots on black background, 22-inch monitor, 57 cm, 800 ms stimulus (two cycles).

---

## Experiment 1 — Synchronicity and Detection

Synchronicity varied from 0 (random phases) to 1 (fully synchronized). Observers reported whether they perceived oscillation or streaming.

**Result:** Detection of oscillation increased monotonically with synchronicity (Spearman Rs = 0.91, p < 0.001). In the fully random-phase condition — where every dot is oscillating — observers were **blind to the oscillation** and perceived only streaming transparent motion.

---

## Experiment 2 — Streaming Strength Suppresses Oscillation Detection

Superimposed a coherent streaming motion signal (variable coherence: 5%, 10%, 20%, 90%) on top of synchronized oscillating dots (which would normally be detected easily). Observers reported oscillation presence/absence (160 trials).

**Result:** Oscillation detection decreased monotonically as streaming coherence increased (Rs = 0.92, p < 0.001). At 90% streaming coherence, oscillation detection was severely impaired even when dots were synchronized (normally easy to detect). As streaming strength weakened, detection recovered.

---

## Key Conclusions

1. **Global surface representations suppress locally inconsistent signals.** When the streaming percept is strong, the brain discards the local oscillatory behavior of individual dots — it is literally invisible. The global wins over the local.

2. **The mechanism is hierarchical feedback suppression.** The authors propose that higher areas (MT, representing the global streaming surfaces) send feedback to early areas (V1), suppressing the representation of locally inconsistent motion signals. This parallels motion-induced blindness and motion capture.

3. **Surface perception does not require — and actively ignores — local dot trajectory consistency.** Two vivid transparent surfaces are constructed from statistically organized oscillating elements, even though no individual element is moving in the direction of either perceived surface.

---

## Why This Is Important for VRDots

**Direct relevance to the Dots50Swap result.** In VRDots, swapping 50% of dots between the two fields at tStart did not reduce the cueing advantage (+34.4pp, essentially unchanged from the no-swap baseline of +30.4pp). The Kanai et al. result provides a mechanism: the global surface representations are built from population-level motion energy statistics, not from tracking individual dot identities. Swapping half the dots leaves the population statistics of both fields intact — the same two direction-energy channels are present before and after the swap — so the surface percept (and the attentional hold on the cued surface) is unaffected.

**VRDots as an extension.** Kanai et al. showed that random-phase oscillation (zero net motion) still produces transparent streaming. VRDots uses dots that DO have coherent net motion (actual translation). The Kanai result sets a lower bound: even when local motion is entirely oscillatory, the global surface percept is robust. VRDots operates well above this threshold.

**For the motivation paragraph:** The Kanai et al. result demonstrates that whatever the brain is tracking to maintain the cued-surface representation across the pre-translation delay, it is NOT the specific set of individual dot trajectories. This makes the question of what *is* being tracked — and whether depth-plane membership is part of it — all the more interesting.
